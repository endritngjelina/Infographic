Industrial Revolution Era

The foundation of modern technology began with steam and steel

1760 - 1840

Steam Power

James Watt's steam engine revolutionized manufacturing and transportation, marking the beginning of mechanized industry.

1825

Railway System

The first passenger railway connected distant cities, shrinking the world and enabling mass transportation of goods and people.

1844

Telegraph

Samuel Morse's telegraph system enabled instant long-distance communication, laying the groundwork for global connectivity.

1879

Electric Light

Edison's incandescent bulb illuminated the world, extending productive hours and transforming urban life forever.

Electronics Revolution

The dawn of electronic communication and computation

1876

Telephone

Alexander Graham Bell's telephone enabled voice communication across distances, connecting people like never before.

1901

Radio Waves

Marconi's wireless transmission crossed the Atlantic, proving that radio waves could carry information globally.

1947

Transistor

The invention of the transistor at Bell Labs laid the foundation for all modern electronic devices and computers.

1969

ARPANET

The precursor to the internet connected four universities, beginning the age of networked computing.

Computer Revolution

From room-sized machines to personal computers

1946

ENIAC

The first general-purpose electronic computer weighed 30 tons and filled an entire room, yet could perform 5,000 calculations per second.

1971

Microprocessor

Intel's 4004 chip put the power of a computer on a single silicon chip, making personal computers possible.

1984

Personal Computer

The Apple Macintosh introduced the graphical user interface to mainstream computing, making computers accessible to everyone.

1991

World Wide Web

Tim Berners-Lee's invention transformed the internet from a research tool into a global information superhighway.
